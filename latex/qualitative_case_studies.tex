\subsection{Qualitative Trade-offs and Failure Analysis}
\label{appendix:case_diagnostics}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We analyze five examples from the C-MuMOInstruct \texttt{test}
split (\texttt{instr\_setting=seen}), comparing GeLLM$^4$O-C
(SFT), GRPO, and GDPO on Mistral, with one additional MolT5
SFT/GRPO/GDPO example. These are \emph{single greedy-candidate}
outputs, not the main beam-20 evaluation; Mistral uses seed 42,
while the MolT5 generation seed was not recorded.
Success and property-level pass/fail status were recomputed
using the benchmark property calculators and source-dependent
$\Delta_p$ rules. ADMET results are predictions, not
experimental observations. Structural similarity is Morgan
radius-2, 2048-bit Tanimoto; no hard similarity constraint
is enforced in this evaluation. Cases are selected to expose
contrasting mechanisms, not to estimate their prevalence.

\paragraph{Constraint satisfaction versus stronger targeted gains.}
In \textbf{BCMQ \#19 (Mistral)}, GRPO satisfies all criteria,
whereas GDPO increases predicted CARC from 0.1345 to 0.4112
and MUTAG from 0.1462 to 0.2612; these exceed the
allowed preservation degradations of 0.2 and 0.1,
respectively. The GeLLM$^4$O-C baseline also violates
CARC preservation (0.5348) and fails the QED improvement margin.
GRPO predicts CARC=0.0186 and MUTAG=0.0768,
preserving both constraints. Conversely, in
\textbf{ACEP \#127}, the source CARC=0.3452 requires
a decrease of at least 0.2, to at most 0.1452.
The baseline (0.2071) and GRPO (0.1636) fall short,
but GDPO attains 0.0615 and succeeds. Thus,
joint satisfaction can depend on preserving a weak
dimension or making enough progress on a still-failing
target; neither variant uniformly resolves both.

\paragraph{Both succeed, with different optimization emphases.}
The \textbf{ABMP \#208 (Mistral)} input is especially
informative because the base, GRPO, and GDPO candidates
all satisfy SOR (but not SSOR), yet the two RL variants
generate different molecules
(see the corresponding molecular structure comparison).
The baseline predicts AMP=0.9099 and BBBP=0.5551.
GDPO produces larger predicted gains in AMP
(0.8026 to 0.9413 versus 0.9117 under GRPO)
and BBBP (0.4084 to 0.6891 versus 0.5737).
GRPO, however, achieves a lower predicted MUTAG
(0.0821 versus 0.1343) and retains more of the
source structure (Tanimoto 0.7246 versus 0.5600).
All four source-conditioned criteria pass for both
models. This observation is consistent with the
aggregate distinction between stronger joint-criterion
satisfaction under GRPO and larger relative property
improvements under GDPO, but a single example does not
establish a general algorithmic preference.

\paragraph{Why do the models fail?}
In \textbf{ABMP \#18 (Mistral)}, both candidate SMILES
are valid yet both fail SOR for different reasons.
GRPO lowers MUTAG from 0.3387 to 0.2728, an improvement
of only 0.0659 versus the required 0.1 decrease.
GDPO lowers MUTAG much further to 0.0504, but
also reduces BBBP from 0.9099 to 0.6743,
exceeding the maximum allowed 0.1 degradation.
This illustrates a trade-off between aggressive
target-property optimization and protecting a
preservation constraint. It is not evidence of a
universal tendency on every input.

\paragraph{Structural drift despite successful scoring.}
In \textbf{ABMP \#417 (MolT5)}, GDPO reaches SOR=SSOR=1,
with predicted AMP=0.9988, BBBP=0.9540,
MUTAG=0.0005, and PlogP=4.0041, but produces
a substituted benzene with Tanimoto similarity of
only 0.0476 to the multi-ring, heteroatom-rich source.
The SFT and GRPO variants still miss the required
MUTAG improvement (predicted values 0.6472 and 0.3814).
Here, the scoring criteria succeed while scaffold
preservation is poor, exposing a limitation of
property-only threshold evaluation, not experimentally
validated drug quality.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
The cases identify concrete failure mechanisms---insufficient
improvement on a required property, violation of a preserved
property, and substantial structure changes despite scored success.
They also show that equal SOR can hide different molecular edits
and property-level improvements. Such diagnostic observations
complement, but do not replace, the aggregate benchmark evidence.
