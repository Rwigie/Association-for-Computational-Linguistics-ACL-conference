\subsection{Qualitative Trade-offs and Failure Analysis}
\label{appendix:case_diagnostics}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We analyze five examples from the C-MuMOInstruct \texttt{test}
split (\texttt{instr\_setting=seen}), comparing GeLLM$^4$O-C
(SFT), GRPO, and GDPO on Mistral, with one additional MolT5
SFT/GRPO/GDPO example. These are \emph{single greedy-candidate}
outputs, not the main beam-20 evaluation; Mistral uses seed 42,
while the MolT5 generation seed was not recorded.
Success and property-level pass/fail status were recomputed
using the benchmark property calculators and source-dependent
$\Delta_p$ rules. ADMET results are predictions, not
experimental observations. Structural similarity is Morgan
radius-2, 2048-bit Tanimoto; no hard similarity constraint
is enforced in this evaluation. Cases are selected to expose
contrasting mechanisms, not to estimate their prevalence.

\subsection{Different edits despite equal SOR on ABMP}
\label{sec:case_abmp208}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figs/cmoral_case_abmp208.pdf}
\caption{An ABMP source molecule and the SFT, GRPO, and GDPO outputs produced by Mistral-7B under single-candidate decoding. Labels report predicted AMP and BBBP scores and changes relative to the same source, with MUTAG and Morgan similarity where available. All three candidates satisfy SOR but not SSOR, yet exhibit different molecular edits and property improvements.}
\label{fig:case_abmp208}
\end{figure*}
Figure~\ref{fig:case_abmp208} directly compares the verified molecular structures
and property values, showing different editing strategies even when the success
indicator is the same. The baseline,
GRPO and GDPO all satisfy SOR but fail SSOR.
Relative to the source AMP=0.8026 and BBBP=0.4084,
GDPO reaches AMP=0.9413 and BBBP=0.6891, compared with
AMP=0.9117 and BBBP=0.5737 under GRPO. In contrast,
GRPO attains the lower predicted MUTAG (0.0821 versus 0.1343)
and better structural similarity (Morgan Tanimoto 0.7246 versus 0.5600).
The baseline predicts AMP=0.9099 and BBBP=0.5551.
Thus equal joint success can mask meaningful differences in
property improvement and scaffold preservation.

\subsection{Joint constraint satisfaction on BCMQ}
\label{sec:case_bcmq}
\IfFileExists{figs/cmoral_case_bcmq19.pdf}{%
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figs/cmoral_case_bcmq19.pdf}
\caption{BCMQ molecular editing by the SFT baseline, \textsc{C-Moral}-GRPO and \textsc{C-Moral}-GDPO. Property values are source-conditional model predictions; successful and violated constraints should be highlighted per candidate.}
\label{fig:case_bcmq19}
\end{figure*}
}{}
For this representative BCMQ input, GRPO satisfies all required criteria. The
SFT baseline and GDPO do not. In particular, GDPO raises predicted CARC
from 0.1345 to 0.4112 and MUTAG from 0.1462 to 0.2612, exceeding
their permitted preservation margins of 0.2 and 0.1, respectively.
The SFT baseline likewise violates CARC preservation (0.5348)
and misses the QED improvement target. GRPO achieves CARC=0.0186
and MUTAG=0.0768, preserving both constraints. The successful
candidate therefore improves joint satisfaction not merely by
maximizing a favorable property but by avoiding two constraint violations.

\subsection{A carcinogenicity-improvement bottleneck on ACEP}
\label{sec:case_acep}
\IfFileExists{figs/cmoral_case_acep127.pdf}{%
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figs/cmoral_case_acep127.pdf}
\caption{ACEP optimization: source and generated molecular structures, predicted properties and threshold satisfaction for SFT, GRPO and GDPO.}
\label{fig:case_acep127}

}{}
The source molecule has predicted CARC=0.3452. The requested improvement
requires a reduction of at least 0.2, hence CARC must be at most 0.1452.
The baseline (0.2071) and GRPO (0.1636) both improve CARC but
fall short of the prescribed threshold. GDPO achieves 0.0615
and meets this constraint. This example illustrates that a candidate
can make a substantial improvement and still fail the specified
source-dependent objective.

\subsection{Preservation versus improvement on ABMP}
\label{sec:case_abmp18}
\IfFileExists{figs/cmoral_case_abmp18.pdf}{%
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figs/cmoral_case_abmp18.pdf}
\caption{ABMP failure analysis. GRPO misses the MUTAG improvement margin, whereas GDPO violates a BBBP preservation requirement.}
\label{fig:case_abmp18}
\end{figure*}
}{}
Both models generate valid SMILES but fail SOR for different
reasons. GRPO reduces predicted MUTAG from 0.3387 to 0.2728,
a reduction of only 0.0659 against the required 0.1.
GDPO reduces MUTAG further to 0.0504; however, it also
decreases BBBP from 0.9099 to 0.6743,
exceeding the permitted preservation degradation of 0.1.
The contrast makes clear why improving one objective does not
guarantee success when another must be preserved.

\subsection{Scaffold drift despite property success on ABMP}
\label{sec:case_abmp417}
\IfFileExists{figs/cmoral_case_abmp417.pdf}{%
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figs/cmoral_case_abmp417.pdf}
\caption{MolT5 optimization with large structural drift. Although GDPO meets all measured thresholds, the generated compound is markedly dissimilar to the input.}
\label{fig:case_abmp417}

The MolT5-GDPO output satisfies both SOR and SSOR,
with predicted AMP=0.9988, BBBP=0.9540,
MUTAG=0.0005 and PlogP=4.0041. However, the output is a
substituted benzene and its Morgan Tanimoto similarity to the
heteroatom-rich, multi-ring source is only 0.0476.
The MolT5-SFT and GRPO candidates still miss MUTAG
improvement (predicted values 0.6472 and 0.3814).
This case separates success under calculated property thresholds
from the separate medicinal-chemistry requirement of
preserving a useful starting scaffold.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Taken together, these molecule-level comparisons identify concrete failure mechanisms---insufficient
improvement on a required property, violation of a preserved
property, and substantial structure changes despite scored success.
They also show that equal SOR can hide different molecular edits
and property-level improvements. Such diagnostic observations
complement, but do not replace, the aggregate benchmark evidence.
