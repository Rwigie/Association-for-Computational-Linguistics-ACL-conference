\subsection{Representative GRPO--GDPO Case Studies}
\label{appendix:case_diagnostics}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We select five contrasting examples from the C-MuMOInstruct
\texttt{test} split (\texttt{instr\_setting=seen}), comparing the
GeLLM$^4$O-C base checkpoint with GRPO/GDPO for Mistral and Llama,
and SFT with GRPO/GDPO for MolT5. Unlike the main beam-20 benchmark,
these examples use \emph{one greedily decoded candidate}
(Mistral/Llama seed 42; MolT5 seed not recorded).
SOR/SSOR and property pass/fail decisions were recalculated using
the test calculators and source-conditional $\Delta_p$ rules.
ADMET values are predictions, not experimental measurements.
Tanimoto similarity uses Morgan radius-2/2048-bit fingerprints,
without a hard similarity cutoff. The cases are deliberately
selected rather than representative of the full test distribution.

\paragraph{Opposite outcomes arise from different bottlenecks.}
In \textbf{BCMQ \#19 (Mistral)}, GRPO satisfies all criteria,
but GDPO raises predicted CARC from 0.1345 to 0.4112 and
MUTAG from 0.1462 to 0.2612, violating preservation
tolerances of 0.2 and 0.1, respectively; GRPO instead
predicts CARC=0.0186 and MUTAG=0.0768.
Conversely, in \textbf{ACEP \#127}, decreasing CARC from
0.3452 requires a score at most 0.1452 ($\Delta=0.2$).
The base (0.2071) and GRPO (0.1636) do not meet this
criterion, whereas GDPO reaches 0.0615. The GDPO
output changes the local carbonyl-linked cyclopropyl
substituent relative to the other candidates.
These differences establish which scored criterion
is decisive, not a causal chemical mechanism.

\paragraph{Different molecules can both satisfy strict success.}
In \textbf{BCMQ \#333 (Llama)}, GRPO and GDPO both
achieve SOR=SSOR=1 but yield different valid SMILES
(Table~\ref{tab:case_smiles_diagnostic}).
The corresponding input-to-output Tanimoto similarities
are 0.4898 and 0.3529, respectively.
Identical binary outcomes therefore need not indicate
equivalent molecular edits or scaffold preservation.

\paragraph{Different failures and a structural limitation.}
In \textbf{ABMP \#18 (Mistral)}, both candidates are valid
but fail for different reasons: GRPO reduces MUTAG from
0.3387 to 0.2728, missing the required 0.1 reduction,
whereas GDPO reduces MUTAG to 0.0504 but degrades BBBP
from 0.9099 to 0.6743, exceeding the allowed 0.1 loss.
In \textbf{ABMP \#417 (MolT5)}, GDPO attains
SOR=SSOR=1 but generates a heavily alkyl-substituted
benzene with Tanimoto similarity only 0.0476 to the
multi-ring source. This illustrates that success on
scored properties does not guarantee meaningful
structural conservation or experimental utility.
